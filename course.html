// Curriculum

WHAT WE
TEACH

Saturday Ultimate Youth Project (UYP) players cover the same content at their own pace. The coach adapts the pacing to the squad. School programmes use the same skills inside a school term.

// Skill areas

WHAT PLAYERS
LEARN

We keep returning to the same skills and go deeper, rather than adding a new system each time. Saturday UYP is for ages 12–20. School programmes are for secondary students. The coach adapts the work for beginners through to players who already compete.

With the disc

Throwing
Passing the disc
A flat backhand and forehand, turning to face the throw, and a pass that curves past the defender guarding the thrower. More experienced players also learn a long throw.
Cutting
Running to get free
A sharp change of direction, moving sideways, timing the run, and using space so a teammate can throw to you.
Catching
Taking the disc
Catching while running, without slowing down. Catching in space, and catching high and low.

Defending

Footwork
Moving well
A ready stance, moving sideways and backwards, a quick first step, and guarding the thrower without making contact. Ultimate is non-contact by rule.
Space
Taking away the easy option
Denying the space the player you guard wants, and closing gaps together as a team.
Positioning
Staying with your player
Staying between the player you guard and the disc, or the end zone they are trying to reach.

On both sides

Decisions
What to do next
On attack, players learn one basic formation: who starts the play, and who runs to get free. They choose a throw before the defender finishes counting to 10. On defense, each player guards one person, the team steers throws toward one side, and players talk when they need to switch.

// Character

SIX
OUTCOMES

We teach, observe and report three outcomes: communication, leadership and critical thinking. We also watch for confidence, initiative and self-awareness. These are observed and reported, not graded. Communication comes first, because a player who can explain their thinking can lead and work out what to do next.

Taught & reported
Communication
Shares their view clearly, listens to the other side of a call, and resolves disagreements with respect. Players practise saying a teammate's name before the call, make and sort out calls in games they referee themselves, and are invited to speak at the end. No one has to speak.
Taught & reported
Leadership
Takes charge when given a role and lifts teammates who are struggling. Players take turns as captain, one player leads part of the warm-up, and players who have been here longer help newer players.
Taught & reported
Critical Thinking
Reads situations from different angles and solves problems instead of dwelling on mistakes. During a game the coach sometimes pauses and asks a player what they see and what they would do next.
Watched & reported
Confidence
Willing to try, and to fail safely, on and off the field. Mistakes are expected in practice, and every player gets time in the game.
Watched & reported
Initiative
Sees what needs doing and steps up without being asked. Some jobs, such as setting up or starting a drill, are left open so a player can step forward.
Watched & reported
Self-Awareness
Knows their strengths and limits, and asks for help when they need it. Players are invited to name what they do well and what to work on next, and the coach talks with them about it.
// How progress is observed and reported

We observe and report each outcome, from "not yet visible" to "established". UYP parents get a monthly progress update. If your child joins through their school, the school gets a summary report each term. The school can also ask for individual notes on each student. Players will have the opportunity to compete in DC Ultimate Frisbee Academy events.

// In a session

THE SAME
FOUR BLOCKS

Every session follows the same four blocks, so players know what to expect. Each block builds a character skill as well as a sport skill.

01
Warm-up
Movement prep and a short activity that brings the group to the same energy. One player leads part of it. This builds communication.
02
Skill practice
Practice that mixes more than one skill at a time. The practice itself teaches the decision, which builds critical thinking.
03
Game play
Small-sided games where players make their own calls, with one player given a captain's challenge. This builds leadership. Every player gets game time.
04
Spirit circle
A short group chat at the end, during stretching. The coach encourages players and invites them to share a reflection. No one has to speak.

// Spirit of the game

PLAYERS
REFEREE THEMSELVES

Ultimate is one of the only sports with no referee, and it is non-contact by rule. Players learn the basic rules needed to get a game going, including how to make a call, listen to the other side, and accept the result.
